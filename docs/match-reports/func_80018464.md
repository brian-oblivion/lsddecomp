## Why this is tractable, not toolchain-blocked, and still deferred

This round's `func_800195EC` (69 instructions, also "Handwritten function")
proved the technique needed here works: no C form exists for `rtpt`/`nclip`/
`avsz3`/`rtps`/`cfc2` in this pinned binutils (confirmed — `as` errors
`unrecognized opcode` on all four "cofun" GTE execute mnemonics, and even
`objdump` disassembles retail's own bytes for them generically), so they go
in as raw `.word` encodings inside whole-block `__asm__` sections, mixed
with ordinary C for everything else. That match report also root-caused and
documents a real `maspsx` behavior (defensive nop-insertion after any
branch/jump mnemonic inside a hand-written asm block, fixed with a tab-
delimited `.set noreorder`/`.set reorder` bracket) that WILL recur here —
`func_80018464`'s ~958 instructions almost certainly contain many branches
inside GTE-adjacent asm regions, so budget for hitting it repeatedly.

At ~14x `func_800195EC`'s size, with GTE and plain-C sections interleaved
throughout rather than segregated (unlike this round's other two GTE
functions, `func_800193C0`/`func_800194A4`, which separated cleanly into a
few `__asm__` blocks around otherwise-ordinary C), this is a multi-round
undertaking on its own, not a single-session target. It should be staffed
deliberately — plan for it as a dedicated round, not a "if there's time
left" queue item, and expect to reuse every lever from
`docs/match-reports/func_800195EC.md`, `func_800193C0.md`, and
`func_800194A4.md` (the `.set noreorder`/`reorder` bracket, dropping bogus
GPR clobbers on `lwc2`/`swc2` blocks, explicit intermediate struct pointers
for reload sequences, `goto`+labeled `return` for early exits whose value
differs from the success path) from word one rather than rediscovering them.
