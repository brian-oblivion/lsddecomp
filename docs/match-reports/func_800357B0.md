## Why this one, and why it stopped here

This function owns a double switch (`jtbl_80010ED8` + `jtbl_80010F38`)
and is exactly the shape the coordinator-verified block-order lever
(source DECLARATION order decides the fallthrough arm, confirmed on
`func_8003424C` and `func_80035B2C` this round) should pay off on twice.
The control flow, both jump tables' index-to-label mappings, and every
case body's OPERATIONS are fully decoded below. What blocks a real
attempt is narrower and specific: **the exact type and role of the 4th
parameter, and the byte-layout of the small scratch struct it seeds**,
because that struct is the SAME one `func_800351D0` builds via a 32-byte
unaligned `lwl`/`lwr`/`swl`/`swr` whole-struct copy (at its own stack
offsets `0x5c..0x77` copied to `0x10..0x2b`, immediately before its call
into this function) and passes here by value. Guessing the struct's
field layout without deriving `func_800351D0`'s own construction of it
first risks a struct that compiles, "matches" by accident on the
easy fields, and is subtly wrong on the ones this function doesn't
happen to exercise -- exactly the shared-struct hazard CLAUDE.md warns
is otherwise invisible until a DIFFERENT, already-matched function
breaks.

## Proposed learning

Filed as a data point for the standing "shared-struct hazard" rule: a
struct doesn't have to be a NAMED shared type across units to carry the
hazard -- a raw stack-passed byte range built by one function's whole-copy
and consumed field-by-field by another, with only ONE of the two ever
attempted, is the same risk under a different name. Deriving the
producer (`func_800351D0`) and the consumer (`func_800357B0`) together,
rather than picking whichever looks smaller in isolation, is probably the
right unit of work next time either is staffed.
